The Paradigm Shift: Rules vs. Data-Driven Computing Day 1 Focus

Traditional Programming (Rule-Based)

Methodology: Humans define explicit logical mathematical formulas and code rules, feed in inputs, and compute outputs.

Input + Explicit Rules $\rightarrow$ Output

Engineering Analogy: Finite Element Analysis (FEA) mesh calculation with explicitly defined boundary conditions.

Machine Learning (Data-Driven)

Methodology: We feed historical input data alongside matching output examples into algorithms to synthesize underlying equations.

Input Data + Historical Outputs $\rightarrow$ Rules (Model)

Engineering Analogy: Empirical System Identification — analyzing raw input-output vibrations to map transfer equations.

Artificial Intelligence (AI)

The overarching field of computer science dedicated to building systems capable of executing cognitive tasks that typically require human reasoning.

A Brief History of AI with Deep Learning Evolution Timeline

  • First Golden Age (1956–1969): Initiated by the Dartmouth workshop. Focused on theorem proving, early neural networks (Perceptron), and symbolic representation.
  • First Dark Age (1969–1980): Triggered by the XOR problem proof, showing simple Perceptrons couldn't solve non-linear operations.
  • Second Golden Age (1980–1995): Driven by Backpropagation (Rumelhart/Hinton) and Neocognitrons. Culminated in SVMs and early CNNs.
  • Second Dark Age (1998–2006): Vanishing gradient limits restricted shallow neural network performance.
  • Third Golden Age (2006–Present): Deep learning scale-ups (AlexNet in 2012) and Transformers in 2017 lead to ChatGPT and modern models.

Vaswani & The Self-Attention Revolution Special Mention

The Landmark Transformer Paper (2017)

In 2017, a team of researchers led by Ashish Vaswani published the seminal paper "Attention Is All You Need", introducing the Transformer architecture.

Previously, models like LSTMs/RNNs processed text sequentially, word-by-word, creating context memory bottlenecks and preventing GPU parallelization.

"The attention mechanism allows the model to dynamically look at and weigh the importance of all other words in a sequence simultaneously, regardless of their distance."

The Mathematical Core

Transformers compute similarity scores between Query (Q), Key (K), and Value (V) matrices:

$$\text{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V$$

Historical Impact

By enabling GPU clusters to train on massive datasets in parallel, Vaswani's work unlocked the scale-up capacity that directly powered GPT-3, ChatGPT, Claude, and all modern foundation models.

Tokenization: The Text-to-Numbers Interface Module 1

Why Simple Splits Fail

Splitting text strictly on blank space characters (string.split()) leads to token replication issues:

  • "love" vs. "love," (contains trailing comma)
  • "me!" vs. "me" (contains exclamation mark)
We must use Regular Expressions (Regex) to cleanly isolate word characters \w+ from individual punctuation symbols.

Batched Regex Tokenization

Real-world language models accept a list of multiple sentences, lowercases them, and parses them simultaneously:

Input: "I love AI, and AI loves me."
Tokens: ['i', 'love', 'ai', ',', 'and', 'ai', 'loves', 'me', '.']

Vocabulary Mapping & Sequence Padding Module 1

Vocabulary & Index Codes

We index every unique token in the corpus. We reserve specific IDs for control signals:

Special Token ID Purpose

<UNK> 1 Represent unknown testing words

Sequence Padding Alignment

Deep Learning models process batches in fixed rectangular tensor shapes. Shorter sentences are padded with zeros, and longer ones are truncated:

Sentence 1: [2, 3, 4, 5, 0, 0]
Sentence 2: [6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11]

Sarveshwar Academy × Knowledge Institute of Technology Module 2 Intro

Word Embeddings: Meaning as Coordinates Module 2

From Indexes to Semantic Vectors

Token IDs are categorical. In a vocab, index 2 (king) and 3 (queen) are geometrically as distinct as 2 (king) and 100 (banana).

An **Embedding Layer** represents each word as a vector of $D$ real numbers (e.g. 512 dimensions in base models). Words with similar meanings cluster together in this high-dimensional space.

Vector Geometry ($v_{\text{king}} - v_{\text{man}} + v_{\text{woman}} \approx v_{\text{queen}}$)

Similar words point in similar directions, sharing semantic vector angles.

Sarveshwar Academy × Knowledge Institute of Technology Module 2 | 01

Self-Attention: The Query, Key, and Value Game Module 2

Intuitive Concept

In the sentence: "The puppy didn't cross the street because it was too tired."

How does the computer map "**it**" to "**puppy**"?

"Through Self-Attention, every word queries all other words in the sentence to calculate a friendship score (Attention weights), combining their representations."

Query (Q): "What am I looking for?"

The active target word's search criteria vector.

Key (K): "What kind of word am I?"

The index tag vector that other words match against.

Value (V): "What content do I contain?"

The actual word meaning features that get shared.

Sarveshwar Academy × Knowledge Institute of Technology Module 2 | 02

Calculating Attention Weights & Math Module 2

The 5 Computational Steps

  1. Linear Projection: Multiply embeddings by projection weights to obtain matrices $Q$, $K$, and $V$.
  2. Similarity Matrix: Matrix-multiply $Q$ and $K^T$ to compare queries and keys.
  3. Scaling Factor: Divide scores by $\sqrt{d_k}$ to prevent gradient explosion.
  4. Softmax Activation: Convert scores to probabilities (weights).
  5. Weighted Output: Multiply weights by $V$.

The Mathematical Engine

$$\text{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V$$

This single equation handles context modeling in modern LLMs.

Sarveshwar Academy × Knowledge Institute of Technology Module 3 Intro

Residual Connections: Bypassing Networks Module 3

The Vanishing Gradient Problem

As we stack dozens of neural network layers, signal updates (gradients) decay to zero during training, causing the model to stop learning.

Solution: Shortcut pipelines that bypass layers, allowing gradients to flow back without decay.

$$\text{Output} = \text{Sublayer}(X) + X$$

Information Preservation

Residuals ensure that the original input representations are not lost during mathematical feature transformations.

Original Embeddings are added directly to the Attention Output matrices.
Sarveshwar Academy × Knowledge Institute of Technology Module 3 | 01

LayerNorm & Feedforward Networks Module 3

Layer Normalization (LayerNorm)

Stabilizes activation signals by ensuring values across feature dimensions maintain a mean of 0 and a variance of 1.

$$\mu = \text{mean}(x) \quad | \quad \sigma^2 = \text{variance}(x)$$ $$x_{\text{norm}} = \frac{x - \mu}{\sqrt{\sigma^2 + \epsilon}}$$

$\epsilon$ is a tiny constant preventing division by zero.

Feedforward Network (FFN)

Self-attention models token relationships. The FFN layer processes each token separately, applying non-linear transformations to learn complex features:

hidden = max(0, x @ W1 + b1)
output = hidden @ W2 + b2

Uses ReLU to filter negative coordinate nodes to zero.
